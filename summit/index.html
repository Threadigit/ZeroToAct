The Annual Summit

One day a year where a year of reading becomes one written plan with your name on it.

Most of the year is reading. One day of it is deciding.

The Summit is the day the year’s intelligence stops being context and becomes a position you have written down. You arrive having read the Annual Outlook. You leave with twelve months of direction in your own words, for your own situation.

Global Eyes. African Stakes.

The decisions that reprice a skill or cancel a project are mostly taken somewhere else. The consequences land here. The Summit reads the world the way it is actually ordered, from where the rules are written down to what it changes for the work you do, and it does not pretend that order runs the other way.

The shape of the day

One day, virtual, with the Outlook as the text. The morning establishes where the economy is heading and why. The afternoon turns that into your plan, because a forecast you did not write anything down against is entertainment.

Three answers, not one

Where you stand decides what a shift means for you. The same reading of the year produces a different move depending on which of these you are.

  • No skill yetWhich skills survive where the economy is heading, matched to what you can realistically learn and stay with.
  • Building or running somethingWhere the openings are in the year ahead, and how to take them from where you actually stand.
  • Putting money to workWhere to be looking next, and what the cost of borrowing does to the case for waiting.

What you leave with

A written twelve-month plan with your name on it, and a cell of five who will ask you about it every two weeks.

The plan is the deliverable. Not notes, not a recording. The Summit is built around the document you walk out holding, and the Live Network exists so that document does not go in a drawer.

Where it sits

The Weekly Signals build the context, free, all year. The Annual Outlook grades the year’s calls and sets a position. The Summit turns that position into your plan. The Live Network holds you to it. Each one is useful alone and they compound in that order.
